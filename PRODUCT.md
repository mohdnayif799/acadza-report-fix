# PRODUCT.md

## Who loses the most when a question is broken

I think the student loses the most. A teacher who meets a broken question can fix it straight away, but a student ends up confused and might blame themselves. The ones who report it lose time explaining the problem, and the ones who don't report it lose even more, because they carry the wrong idea into the exam.

That's why wrong answer keys sit at the top of the queue. They hurt even the students who never tap "Report error".

## What I would never let the AI auto-apply

I would never let the AI change an answer key unless it's independently verified: two different solvers and the stored solution all have to agree. And I wouldn't let it change or invent options.

In R027, a student tried to tell the system to change the answer to C. Our check showed the stored answer was correct, so nothing changed. In Q36, option D was missing completely. The AI could only have guessed it, so it went to a teacher.

## One metric for week 1

How often teachers undo or edit a fix the system applied on its own, as a percentage of all automatic fixes. If that number is low, the fixes can be trusted. If it rises, that type of fix should go back to manual review.

## One idea beyond the brief

Turn every confirmed fix into an automatic check that scans all 8 lakh questions, so the same problem is fixed everywhere before students hit it. In just 43 questions, it found 23 problems nobody had reported.
